The potential of QC in medicine began to emerge more clearly with advances in quantum algorithms and hardware during the 2010s. A major breakthrough was the development of quantum algorithms for molecular simulation, motivated by the difficulty of classically modelling complex molecular systems and thus opening the possibility of applying QC to drug discovery. Another promising direction is the emergence of Quantum Machine Learning (QML), with applications to genomic data analysis, medical diagnostics and personalised medicine{% cite Chow2024 %}.
These developments have established healthcare as a promising area for quantum computing. However, current quantum technologies remain limited by hardware constraints, noise and scalability, making the integration of QC into medical applications an ongoing challenge {% cite Chow2024 %}. This report explores this emerging intersection between quantum computing and medicine, with a core emphasis on the development of quantum simulation methods for the study of molecular systems.
