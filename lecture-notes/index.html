lecture notes

Notes on Mathematics and Quantum Sciences.

My lecture notes from the Master in Applied Mathematics, with a minor in Quantum Science & Computing, at EPFL. A word of warning ! While I am happy with these lecture notes, some are still a work in progress and would benefit from further revision. Also, they almost surely contain typos. That said, I still think there is plenty in them worth reading. – Arthur, October 2026.

Mathematics

Course / PDF Academic year Description
Numerical Integration of Stochastic Differential Equations Fall 2024 A complete course on numerical methods for stochastic differential equations, including an introductory section on stochastic calculus.
Statistical Analysis of Networks Data Fall 2024 A course on random graph models, and statistics for networks.
Lattice Models Fall 2024 A first course on Lattice Models. Warning ! These notes require revision.
Martingales in Financial Mathematics Spring 2025 A detailed course on Martingales and Stochastic Calculus for Financial Mathematics, in both discrete and continuous-time.
Multivariate Statistics Spring 2025 A complete course on Statistics for random vectors and matrices, including inference, dimension reduction, and Gaussian graphical models.

Quantum Science & Information Theory

Course / PDF Academic year Description
Introduction to Quantum Computation Spring 2025 An introductory course on Quantum Computing, basic algorithms, and Quantum Error Correction.
Quantum Information Theory Spring 2025 A (relatively) detailed course on Quantum Information Theory. The first few chapters require more details.
Statistical Mechanics Spring 2025 A course on Statistical Mechanics from the point of view of Materials Science. Knowledge of Quantum Mechanics and Particle Physics is advised.
Information Theory & Coding Fall 2026 A detailed course on Information Theory and its implications for Coding Theory (data compression, transmission of information).

I also took some courses in Multiscale Stochastic Analysis, Data Science & Machine Learning, and Stochastic Simulation, for which I did not write personal lecture notes.
